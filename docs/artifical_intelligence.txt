Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a broad branch of computer science dedicated to building systems capable of simulating human intelligence, cognitive processes, and decision-making capabilities. The primary objective of AI is to enable machines to learn from experience, adapt to new inputs, and perform tasks traditionally requiring human intellect. Today, AI applications span across multiple domains, including medical diagnostics, financial forecasting, autonomous vehicles, and smart virtual assistants.

AI is generally categorized into two main paradigms: Narrow AI (Weak AI) and General AI (Strong AI or AGI). All current real-world applications fall under the category of Narrow AI, meaning they are designed and trained to excel at a specific task—such as playing chess, recognizing faces, or translating languages. General AI, on the other hand, represents a hypothetical future technology capable of flexible human-like intelligence, allowing it to understand and solve unfamiliar problems independently.

Constructing an AI system involves various methodologies, ranging from rule-based symbolic logic and expert systems to data-driven approaches. Unlike traditional software development where explicit rules are manually coded by engineers, modern AI systems analyze massive datasets to discover underlying patterns and automatically formulate their own logic.

At the core of this data-driven shift lie subfields such as Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL). These specialized disciplines within the AI umbrella empower computers to process raw data and generate precise predictions. In today's technology ecosystem, artificial intelligence serves not merely as a data analysis tool, but as a foundational infrastructure for automated decision-making.
